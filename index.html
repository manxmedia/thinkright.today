Team Coaching

Why Team Coaching

Have you noticed the following dynamics in your team:

  • Lack of trust between your team members?
  • Communication breakdown within the team?
  • Is the team struggling to meet the goals and objectives set by the organisation?
  • Do all team members voice their thoughts and opinions in meetings?
  • Are there team members that don’t voice their thoughts and opinions often or at all?
  • Has productivity decreased within the team?
  • Are team members content and engaged?
  • Have any team members expressed that they’re not happy in their role or at work?
  • Team members avoiding meetings or calling in sick?

If you’ve noticed three or more of these symptoms, then perhaps it’s time to invest in some team coaching.

Think Right Today’s Team Coaching is a strategic service devised to tackle and resolve team-based challenges within an organisation. By assessing specific concerns and originating customised development programmes, our coaching assist teams to enhance their operational effectiveness and productivity. From promoting a shared vision to implementing targeted development plans, Think Right Today team coaching helps to bridge communication gaps between managers and their teams, align team members with organisational culture and values, and facilitate opportunities for synergistic collaboration across the organisation.

At Think Right Today our team coaching approach help teams establish honest and authentic communication; collaborate successfully on projects; maintain accountability and meeting deadlines, demonstrate independence and proactivity in tasks; handle workplace stress and conflict; become innovating and solution focused; foster and strengthen workplace relationships; remaining motivated and engaged.

What Team Coaching Isn’t

Team building: Team building tends to focus on improving interpersonal relationships, productivity and alignment with an organisation’s goals. It typically consists of short, often one-day interventions. In contrast, team coaching is considered to typically take place over a series of sessions.

Team facilitation: While a coach may at times use facilitation skills, facilitation can be considered a way of helping a team manage their dialogue, compared to team coaching, which aims to empower a team to take ownership for their own dialogue.

Team coaching vs. individual coaching (or 1:1 coaching)

Team coaching is distinct from other forms of coaching.

Individual performance is dependent on team climate and environment, and high individual performance is not always commensurate with high team performance. Often, positive changes to individual behaviour are lost long-term if the environment is not supportive, or indeed, high performers leave the organisation altogether.

  • Whilst 1:1 coaching focuses on the individual, team coaching aligns individual priorities with team priorities, making the link between individual and team more conscious. It focuses on the system of which individuals are part, and situates learning within a team context in order to deliver performance improvements.

  • Team coaching is a holistic method that drives lasting change. It acknowledges that teams are dynamic systems with their own values, standards, touch points and complex rules of behaviour that are likely to be unknown to outsiders – in short, that they are more than a collection of individuals.

What are the limitations of team coaching?

  • Team coaching only works for 'real teams', not for pseudo-teams. (Pseudo-teams are groups who work together and appear to be a team, but in fact do not share objectives and a purpose.) In this context, team coaching is unlikely to be effective in yielding sustainable performance improvements.

  • Teams need to have a desire for 'collective improvement'. In other words, in order to be effective, the will to learn from team coaching and introduce sustained change needs to come from within and cannot be imposed from without.

[Team coaching] enabl[es] a team to function at more than the sum of its parts, by clarifying its mission and improving its external and internal relationships. It is different therefore from coaching team leaders on how to lead their teams, or coaching individuals in a group setting."

– Hawkins and Smith, 2006

How does life coaching differ from counselling/therapy?

Many people confuse coaching with counselling/therapy or mentoring and can often use the terms interchangeably. While all these practices revolve themselves around personal development, However, where each practice differs is in terms of scope and methodology.

The Merriam-Webster dictionary defines counselling as “professional guidance of the individual by utilising psychological methods, especially in collecting case history data, using various techniques of the personal interview, and testing interests and aptitudes.” Hence, counselling involves a more emotions-based, problem-specific evaluation of one’s history to provide specific and appropriate guidance. In counselling you and your counsellor spend time examining your past, evaluating how it affects your present behaviour, responses, perspectives, and actions. Through the counselling process, you attempt to work through and heal past trauma and issues to provide mental and emotional well-being in the present.

Numerous people who have been through counselling or therapy often realise that they need a practical approach to apply all the knowledge and understanding that they have accumulated over time. They want to figure out ways to effectively structure their lives, work out their purpose and what they want to achieve with their lives. At this point in their self-development journey, people want tailormade strategies unique to them and their circumstances that will help them to become the people they long to be and live out the future desire. This is where coaching comes in.

Coaching is an equal partnership between a coach and a client where the client directs the process and drives the direction of the conversation and session. A coaching session focuses solely on the outcome the client desires for. A coaches role is to facilitate their client’s forward movement towards their goals, dreams and desired future. A successful coaching session is one where a client is empowered to uncover and develop their own unique path and strategy to achieving their goal and attain their desired future. Thus, coaching is always oriented towards the future. Life coaching is about taking you from your present reality and moving you towards your desired future, not by digging into you past to shed light into your present, but by journeying into your future, understanding what it is exactly that you want from it, and then working out practical and effective strategies in the present that will help you get there.

Therapy and counselling focus on a person’s healing through an examination of their past, whilst coaching is more performance driven to practically move a person forward into living out the life that want. Even though coaching has many therapeutic elements to it like counselling/therapy, but unlike counselling, coaching does not offer therapeutic outcomes. Coaching is an interactive process where you are given the space to try new things, make note of positive and negative outcomes, reflect on your plans and activities, remain focused on your pursuits, document your actions and insights, evaluate your progress, acknowledge and celebrate your accomplishments. There is a lot to do and focus on during and after your coaching sessions which can feel hard and be very challenging, but it will all be worth it in the end!

What is the difference between coaching and mentoring?

While coaching and mentorship have similar aims in some ways such as helping you recognise and understand your abilities and aptitudes, improve your personal and professional performance, and promote a growth mindset. The results of engaging in either a coaching partnership or a mentoring relationship are also similar in enhancing self-confidence, mental agility, resilience, broadening your perspective, and in some instances expand your professional network.

However, mentoring is a more directed, and often more beneficial exchange for the person being mentored, long-term relationship between a more experienced mentor and an inexperienced mentee. The purpose of the relationship is for long-term personal and or career development of the mentee where the mentor does most of the talking and offers advice. This is in contrast to coaching which is a true partnership of equals between the coach and the client. Mentoring most typically consists of a more experienced person or mentor intentionally and directly guiding the mentee through an experience that the mentor has already been through.
